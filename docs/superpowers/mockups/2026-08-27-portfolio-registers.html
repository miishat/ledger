What ships today

Three equal-weight stat cards, a donut with a plain text legend, then one table per account. Nothing is broken and nothing is emphasised. Every number carries the same visual weight as every other number, so the page has no entry point: your eye lands nowhere in particular.

Quiet

One number leads; everything else defers to it. The three cards collapse into a single summary band where Holdings Value is set at display scale and Invested, mix, and count sit beside it as hairline-separated satellites. B

Instrument

Encode everything; make the page readable without reading it. The donut is retired in favour of stacked bars, one per allocation mode, all three visible at once instead of hidden behind a toggle. Rows carry a performance stripe, mono numerals, and a tighter rhythm. This is the densest option and the one that most changes the page's character: it reads like an instrument panel rather than a document.

C

Editorial

Hierarchy through scale and space, not through more ink. The hero splits: a very large value figure on the left, a short derived-facts column on the right (best and weakest performer, currency split, top weight). Allocation becomes a weighted treemap, which reads share far faster than a donut at eight slices. The table sheds two columns to a per-row detail, so the default view is calmer than any other option here.

Instrument's allocation, Editorial's holdings. Where I would go

C for the top of the page, A for the table. The hero split and the treemap are what actually make the page feel richer, and they are additive: nothing existing has to be deleted to get them. The table, meanwhile, is where your test surface and your accessibility guards live, so it wants A's restraint rather than C's disclosure rows or B's stripes. That combination gets most of the visual ceiling for roughly medium risk.

B is the one I would argue against unless you want the app to change character. Retiring the donut costs you a control your e2e suite asserts on, and putting all six chart colours on screen at once in six themes is a contrast problem you would be solving repeatedly.

On D: it works, and the two halves balance better than I expected, a dense read up top over a calm ledger below. Be aware of what it stacks up, though. It is the only register that takes on both heavy test risks at once: the retired allocation toggle from B and the two-state row from C. If you want D, the cheap move is to land the table half first, since it stands alone, then replace the allocation block as a second change rather than carrying both through one review.

One thing first, whichever you pick. Manual price overrides are stamped as USD in the market data service, so the live Portfolio tab currently shows $112,582 where the true figure is $93,394. Every mockup above uses the corrected numbers. Building a richer presentation on top of the uncorrected ones would just make a wrong total more prominent.
